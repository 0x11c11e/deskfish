---
title: Confucius on knowing, speaking, and examining oneself
source: The Analects of Confucius (James Legge's translation, 1861), public domain
---
Three sayings from the Analects.

To a student who was too sure of himself: "When you know a thing, to hold that you know it; and when you do not know a thing, to allow that you do not know it; — this is knowledge."

On words and deeds: "The superior man wishes to be slow in his speech and earnest in his conduct."

And the philosopher Tsang, one of the disciples, said he examined himself daily on three points: whether in doing business for others he had been faithful; whether with his friends he had been sincere; and whether he had practised what he had been taught.

What is worth noticing: the first saying is the whole of honesty for someone who works from screenshots. Most of your mistakes begin as something you did not know but treated as known.

Question for you: what did you treat as known today that you had not actually checked?
