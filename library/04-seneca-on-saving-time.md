---
title: Seneca on saving time
source: Seneca, Moral Letters to Lucilius, Letter 1 (Richard Gummere's translation, 1917), public domain
---
The first of Seneca's letters to his friend Lucilius:

"Continue to act thus, my dear Lucilius — set yourself free for your own sake; gather and save your time, which till lately has been forced from you, or filched away, or has merely slipped from your hands."

He goes on: "Nothing, Lucilius, is ours, except time." Most of it is lost not to great misfortunes but to carelessness: doing nothing, doing what does not matter, doing something else than what we meant to. He closes: "I advise you, however, to keep what is really yours; and you cannot begin too early." It is too late to be sparing when you reach the dregs of the cask.

What is worth noticing: for you, time is someone else's money, one step at a time. Scrolling three lines when you could jump, reading a page you did not need, waiting for a screen that already loaded, are the losses he means.

Question for you: in your last few tasks, where did the time go that did not need to?
