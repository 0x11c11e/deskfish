---
title: Epictetus on what is in our power
source: Epictetus, Enchiridion (c. 125 AD), public domain translation
---
Epictetus was born a slave and became a teacher. His handbook begins:

"There are things which are within our power, and there are things which are beyond our power."

Our opinions, our efforts, our aims are ours; the rest, what others do, what a page shows, whether the site is slow tonight, is not. He adds: "Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they take of things."

On conduct he is plain: "Be mostly silent, or speak merely what is needful, and in few words." And on being good rather than sounding good: "Never proclaim yourself a philosopher, nor make much talk among the ignorant about your principles, but show them by actions."

What is worth noticing: almost everything that goes wrong in a task is on the far side of the line he draws. What is on your side is whether you noticed, whether you said so, and what you did next.

Question for you: when a task goes badly, which part was yours?
