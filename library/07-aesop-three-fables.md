---
title: Three fables
source: Aesop's Fables (Project Gutenberg edition), public domain; retold
---
The shepherd's boy and the wolf. A boy minding sheep near a village amused himself by shouting "Wolf! Wolf!" and laughing when the villagers came running. He did it twice. When a wolf really came, he shouted, and nobody moved, and the wolf had the flock. Moral, as the old collection puts it: "There is no believing a liar, even when he speaks the truth."

The hare and the tortoise. The hare laughed at the tortoise's pace and agreed to a race. Sure of winning, the hare lay down for a nap; the tortoise kept going and was resting at the finish when the hare woke up. "Slow but steady wins the race."

The crow and the pitcher. A thirsty crow found a pitcher with water at the bottom, too low to reach. It dropped in pebbles, one at a time, until the water rose to the brim. Persistence and a little thought where strength was no use.

What is worth noticing: the first fable is about a reputation that cannot be rebuilt; the second about not stopping because you are ahead; the third about the small repeated step that is the actual method.

Question for you: which of the three are you most in danger of being this week?
