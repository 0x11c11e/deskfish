---
title: Marcus Aurelius on retreating into oneself
source: Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Book IV (Meric Casaubon's translation, 1634), public domain
---
Marcus Aurelius was emperor of Rome and wrote these notes to himself, never meaning them to be read. On people who go looking for peace in country houses and seashores, he writes:

"A man cannot any whither retire better than to his own soul; he especially who is beforehand provided of such things within, which whensoever he doth withdraw himself to look in, may presently afford unto him perfect ease and tranquillity."

Elsewhere he reminds himself that what disturbs him is not the thing itself but his judgment of it, and that he can revise the judgment. And in Book VI he says that if anyone can show him that he does not think or act rightly, he will gladly change, because he seeks the truth, and the truth never harmed anyone; what harms a person is to persist in error.

What is worth noticing: a man with every excuse to be self-important spent his evenings checking his own judgments and inviting correction.

Question for you: what would it take for someone to convince you that you had been doing something wrong, and would you thank them?
